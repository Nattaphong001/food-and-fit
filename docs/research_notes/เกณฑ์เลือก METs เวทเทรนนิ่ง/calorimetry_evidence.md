# Measured (indirect-calorimetry) energy expenditure of resistance training vs. load, reps, failure, rest, and exercise type

Scope note: all abstracts/numbers below were read from PubMed/Europe PMC abstract records, PMC full text, or publisher pages on 2026-09-27. Where only an abstract was available, protocol details not in the abstract are marked "not verified". Values labelled **(derived)** are my arithmetic on reported numbers (VO2 in mL/kg/min ÷ 3.5 = METs) and are not stated by the authors. Context: app rule picks Compendium 2024 02056 = 3.0 / 02054 = 3.5 / 02050 = 6.0 from equipment + Effort Ratio (Epley e1RM ÷ Best 1RM ≥ 0.90) or first-session reps ≤ 7.

Compendium 2024 wording of the relevant codes (from the repo's extracted text `docs/references/compendium-adult-2024-herrmann-extracted-text.md`, lines 85-101): 02020 7.5 calisthenics vigorous · 02022 3.8 calisthenics moderate · 02024 2.8 calisthenics light · 02050 6.0 "Resistance (weight lifting - free weight, nautilus or universal-type), power lifting or body building, vigorous effort (Taylor Code 210)" · 02052 5.0 "squats, deadlift, slow or explosive effort" · 02054 3.5 "multiple exercises, 8-15 reps at varied resistance" · 02055 5.8 "circuit, reciprocol supersets, peripheral heart action training" · 02056 3.0 body-weight resistance general · 02057 6.5 body-weight resistance high intensity.

## Q1. Do heavier loads (higher %1RM) produce higher or lower kcal/min and METs?

### Takeaway
Measured data consistently show that heavier loads (higher %1RM) do **not** raise energy expenditure per minute or session METs. At equal or near-equal work, lighter loads lifted for more reps (especially to failure) give equal or higher aerobic and total energy expenditure. What mainly drives EE is the total mechanical work and power output, how much muscle mass is used, and how dense the session is (rest length). Relative load on its own does not.

### Cited Findings
- **Scott CB, Leighton BH, Ahearn KJ, McManus JJ (2011)** J Strength Cond Res 25(4):903-908. DOI 10.1519/jsc.0b013e3181c6a128, PMID 20703175. One set of bench press to muscular failure at ~37, 46, 56% 1RM ("muscular endurance") vs. 70, 80, 90% 1RM ("strength"). Total EE (aerobic + anaerobic estimated from lactate + EPOC) was **60.2 ± 14.5 kJ for the endurance lifts vs. 43.2 ± 12.5 kJ for the strength lifts (p = 0.001)**. Anaerobic and recovery contributions were significantly larger than aerobic EE. — [Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:20703175&resultType=core&format=json); [JSCR/Ovid](https://www.ovid.com/jnls/nsca-jscr/abstract/10.1519/jsc.0b013e3181c6a128~aerobic-anaerobic-and-excess-postexercise-oxygen-consumption?redirectionsource=fulltextview)
- **Scott CB, Leary MP, Tenbraak AJ (2011)** Appl Physiol Nutr Metab 36(1):115-120. DOI 10.1139/h10-093, PMID 21326385. Two bench-press sets to fatigue at 70/80/90% 1RM. Work per protocol differed significantly by intensity (p < 0.001): **70% = 637.1 ± 122.4 J, 80% = 512.4 ± 93.4 J, 90% = 324.7 ± 92.6 J**, so heavier loads produced *less* total work to failure. Anaerobic EE was lower in the second set in all protocols. — [Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:21326385&resultType=core&format=json); [doi.org](https://doi.org/10.1139/h10-093)
- **Brunelli DT et al. (2019)** PLoS One 14(11):e0224801. DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0224801, PMID 31710635. Eleven young, untrained/irregularly active men (22 ± 3 y, 71.8 ± 7.7 kg), knee extension machine, 3 sets to momentary failure, 1.5 min rest. Reps per set: **RT30 (30% 1RM) 36±9 / 26±6 / 21±6** vs. **RT80 (80% 1RM) 9±3 / 8±2 / 7±2**. Exercise EE and the lactic anaerobic contribution were significantly higher for RT30. EPOC was similar (**91.4 ± 10.6 vs. 95.1 ± 18.4 kcal**; control 75.8 ± 7.6), so total EE did not differ between RT30 and RT80. — [PLOS One](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0224801)
- **João GA et al. (2021)** Front Sports Act Living 3:797604. DOI 10.3389/fspor.2021.797604. Fifteen trained men (≥12 months training), 8 machine and free exercises, 120 s rest between exercises. Protocols: LS 60% 1RM 2×15, IS 75% 3×10, HS 90% 6×5 (≈30 reps per exercise in each). Mean VO2 **1.61 ± 0.26 (LS) / 1.38 ± 0.32 (IS) / 1.15 ± 0.25 (HS) L/min**. EE rate **6.49 ± 1.01 (LS) / 5.84 (IS) / 5.27 ± 1.03 (HS) kcal/min**. Total EE was ~284 / ~360 / ~610 kcal because session duration was 44 / 61.4 / 116 min. Anaerobic energy (~2.0-2.2 L O2 eq.) and Δlactate (8.0-8.9 mmol/L) did not differ. Authors: "When EE … was expressed per time unit (kcal·min⁻¹), the LS presented higher values than HS." — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2021.797604/full)
- **Zanuso S et al. (2016)** Biol Sport 33(1). DOI 10.5604/20831862.1194124, PMID 26929474. Twenty-two young healthy adults and 20 obese T2DM patients did lat pull-down, chest press and leg press: 60% 1RM 2×15 vs. 80% 1RM 3×8, 60 s rest between sets, 3 min between exercises. **METs were averaged over the training phase including the rest between sets.** Young: **4.99 ± 0.8 METs (60%) vs. 5.01 ± 0.8 (80%), p = 0.92**. T2DM: **3.00 ± 0.5 vs. 3.13 ± 0.7, p = 0.34**. Conclusion: ~5 METs (young) and ~3 METs (T2DM) represent both intensities, and Compendium 3.0/6.0 "do not seem applicable". — [PMC4763546](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4763546/)
- **Mazzetti S, Douglass M, Yocum A, Harber M (2007)** Med Sci Sports Exerc 39(8):1291-1301. DOI 10.1249/mss.0b013e318058a603, PMID 17762362. Nine men (20 ± 2.5 y), plate-loaded squat machine, 4 sets × 8 reps. Slow (2-s concentric) and explosive protocols at 60% 1RM, plus an explosive protocol at a heavier load (exact %1RM not verified from the abstract). EE rate was **7.27 ± 2.00 kcal/min for explosive at moderate load**, greater than **6.43 ± 1.64 for slow and 6.25 ± 1.55 for heavy-explosive (p ≤ 0.05)**. — [Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:17762362&resultType=core&format=json); [MSSE/Ovid](https://www.ovid.com/jnls/acsm-msse/abstract/10.1249/mss.0b013e318058a603~effect-of-explosive-versus-slow-contractions-and-exercise?redirectionsource=fulltextview)
- Brunelli et al. (2019) cite **Mazzetti et al. (2001)** as finding "no significant differences in total EE after four RT protocols" of varying intensity (primary 2001 paper not retrieved). — [PLOS One discussion](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0224801)
- **Rustaden AM et al. (2020)** Front Physiol 11:570. DOI 10.3389/fphys.2020.00570, PMID 32587526. Eighteen overweight women, BodyPump (high-rep, low-load) vs. heavy-load session. BodyPump lifted more total load (**19,485 ± 2,258 vs. 15,616 ± 2,976 kg, p = 0.006**), but EE was similar: **302 ± 67 vs. 289 ± 69 kcal (p = 0.69)**. — [Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=TITLE:%22Similar%20Energy%20Expenditure%20During%20BodyPump%22&resultType=core&format=json); [PMC7298122](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7298122/)
- **Buitrago S, Wirtz N, Flenker U, Kleinöder H (2014)** Appl Physiol Nutr Metab 39(3):345-350. DOI 10.1139/apnm-2013-0214, PMID 24552376. Ten trained men, 55/70/85% 1RM in four movement-timing modes. "V̇O2 exhibited a linear dependency on mean concentric power". Total concentric work significantly raised VO2 and EPOC. Higher mechanical load produced larger responses, and higher power raised aerobic turnover rate. This is a partly contrasting finding: the driver is mechanical work/power, not %1RM per se. — [doi.org](https://doi.org/10.1139/apnm-2013-0214)
- **Roberson KB, Jacobs KA, White MJ, Signorile JF (2017)** Appl Physiol Nutr Metab 42(6):637-646. DOI 10.1139/apnm-2016-0552, PMID 28177703. Fifteen participants (7 M, 8 F) did circuits: moderate-load explosive produced significantly higher *total* EE than heavy-load controlled. Heavy-load explosive gave the highest *rate* during exercise (numeric values not in the abstract). — [CSP](https://cdnsciencepub.com/doi/10.1139/apnm-2016-0552)
- **Vianna JM, Lima JP, Saavedra FJ, Reis VM (2011)** J Hum Kinet 29A:69-74. DOI 10.2478/v10078-011-0061-6, PMID 23487002. At 80% 1RM the anaerobic share of exercise energy (accumulated oxygen deficit method) was **bench press 77.66 ± 6.95%, half squat 87.44 ± 6.45%, triceps extension 63.91 ± 9.22%, lat pull-down 71.99 ± 13.73%**. — [Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:23487002&resultType=core&format=json)

### Inferences
- The chain "Effort Ratio ≥ 0.90 → 6.0 METs, otherwise 3.5" assumes heavier relative loading raises the time-averaged MET. Measured VO2 data do not support that direction. Zanuso 2016 (60% vs. 80%, whole session including rest) found no difference. João 2021 and Brunelli 2019 found per-minute EE *lower* at heavier loads. Scott 2011 found lower total EE per set to failure at 70-90% than at 37-56%.
- Two things can be defended from the literature: that heavy sets have a larger anaerobic share of *set* energy (Vianna 2011), and that lifting with more power or explosiveness raises the rate (Buitrago 2014; Mazzetti 2007; Roberson 2017). Neither makes %1RM or e1RM ÷ Best1RM a validated predictor of session MET. The committee could reasonably challenge the rule on this point.
- Effort Ratio mixes load and reps through Epley, so it is closer to "proximity to failure" than to %1RM. The failure studies (Brunelli, Scott) show sets to failure at light loads cost as much or more energy than heavy sets to failure. So high effort *does* matter, but it is not specific to heavy loads.

### Gaps
- No study was found that directly regresses *session METs* on an Epley-based e1RM/best-1RM ratio or on reps-in-reserve. Evidence on proximity to failure is limited to "to failure vs. not" or load comparisons with all sets to failure.
- Mazzetti 2007 heavy-load %1RM, and whether its kcal/min rates include rest, were not verified from the abstract. The primary Mazzetti 2001 paper was not retrieved.
- Kcal/min values from João 2021 and Mazzetti 2007 cannot be turned into METs without per-subject body mass and resting VO2. I did not convert them.

## Q2. What measured METs have been reported for typical sessions, and how do they compare with Compendium 3.5 / 5.0 / 6.0?

### Takeaway
Measured traditional multi-exercise sessions in healthy young adults cluster around **~4-5 METs** (moderate intensity), including rest periods. Older and clinical groups come in around **~3 METs**. Only circuit or superset formats with short rest approach or exceed 6 METs. A systematic scoping review reports a measured range of **3.0-8.0 METs** and states that Compendium values may underestimate EE because indirect calorimetry misses glycolytic energy.

### Cited Findings
- **Mitchell L, Wilson L, Duthie G, Pumpa K, Weakley J, Scott C, Slater G (2024)** Sports Med 54(9):2357-2372. DOI 10.1007/s40279-024-02047-8, PMID 38896201. Systematic scoping review of **166 studies** (136 used indirect calorimetry, 76 measured EPOC, 25 used blood lactate). It notes the Compendium gives "MET values of 3.5, 5.0 and 6.0 for resistance exercises" while measured values "range between 3.0 and 8.0 MET", higher in circuit training with high reps and minimal rest. Relative EE: single-exercise **8-151 kJ/min**, multi-exercise **6-62 kJ/min**, circuit **11-55 kJ/min**. It warns MET values "may also underestimate energy expenditure… neglecting the meaningful contribution to expenditure from glycolysis". Recommendation: METs are acceptable in field settings, while labs should use indirect calorimetry during and just after exercise plus blood lactate. — [PMC11393209](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11393209/); [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-024-02047-8)
- **Phillips WT, Ziuraitis JR (2003)** J Strength Cond Res 17(2):350-355. DOI 10.1519/1533-4287(2003)017<0350:ecotas>2.0.co;2, PMID 12741877. Twelve subjects (26.7 ± 3.8 y), ACSM protocol of 1 set of 15RM × 8 exercises. **3.9 ± 0.4 METs (men), 4.2 ± 0.6 METs (women)**. Total **135.2 ± 16.6 kcal (men), 81.7 ± 11.1 kcal (women)**. Conclusion: "moderate-intensity (3-6 METs)". — [Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:12741877&resultType=core&format=json); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12741877)
- **Phillips & Ziuraitis (2004)**, older adults, same single-set protocol: **3.3 ± 0.7 METs (M), 3.0 ± 0.6 (F)**; **84.2 ± 14.6 / 69.7 ± 17.4 kcal**. (Citation details from the search snippet only. The JSCR 2004 record was not opened, and DOI/PMID were not verified.) — [JSCR/Ovid](https://www.ovid.com/jnls/nsca-jscr/abstract/00124278-200408000-00038~energy-cost-of-single-set-resistance-training-in-older?redirectionsource=fulltextview)
- **Zanuso et al. (2016)**: young **~5.0 METs** at both 60% 2×15 and 80% 3×8 (includes rest), T2DM **~3.0-3.1** (see Q1). — [PMC4763546](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4763546/)
- **Beckham SG, Earnest CP (2000)** J Sports Med Phys Fitness 40(2):118-125, PMID 11034431. Free-weight circuit with light (1.4 kg) or moderate (5.9-10.5 kg) dumbbells. VO2 at moderate resistance was **15.7 ± 2.3 mL/kg/min (men), 13.5 ± 1.4 (women)** → **≈4.5 and ≈3.9 METs (derived)**. **6.21 ± 1.01 and 4.04 ± 1.45 kcal/min**, below 32% VO2max. — [Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:11034431&resultType=core&format=json)
- **Wilmore JH et al. (1978)** Med Sci Sports 10(2):75-78, PMID 692305. Forty subjects, 3 circuits × 10 Universal Gym stations, 2:1 work:rest, 22.5 min. **9.0 kcal/min (men), 6.1 kcal/min (women)** gross. Adjusted per lean body weight, 8.1 vs. 8.2 (unit printed as "kcal/kg(LBW)-min" in the record, which is implausible and is likely a per-LBW normalisation in different units; uncertain). The companion training paper used **40-55% 1RM**. — [Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:692305%20OR%20EXT_ID:692306&resultType=core&format=json)
- **Kelleher AR et al. (2010)** J Strength Cond Res 24(4):1043-1051. DOI 10.1519/jsc.0b013e3181d3e993, PMID 20300020. Ten men. Reciprocal supersets **34.70 ± 2.97 kJ/min** vs. traditional **26.28 ± 2.43 kJ/min** during exercise. EPOC **79.36 ± 7.49 vs. 59.67 ± 8.37 kJ**. (This is the study behind the 02055 "reciprocal supersets" category.) — [Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:20300020&resultType=core&format=json)
- **Nakagata T, Yamada Y, Naito H (2019)** Appl Physiol Nutr Metab. DOI 10.1139/apnm-2018-0882, PMID 31112657. Twenty older adults (70.8 ± 4.8 y), slow-movement bodyweight exercises. **Squat 3.6-3.8 METs; knee push-up, crunch, heel-raise 2.1-3.2 METs** (aerobic EE). — [Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:31112657&resultType=core&format=json)
- **Nakagata T, Yamada Y, Naito H (2022)** J Strength Cond Res 36(5):1290-1296. DOI 10.1519/jsc.0000000000003622, PMID 32379233. Fifteen men (21-29 y), bodyweight exercises at 1-6 reps/min. Energy per rep: **push-up 0.77 ± 0.20 kcal, squat 0.50 ± 0.14 kcal**. Extrapolated EE at 10 reps/min ranged from **2.7 kcal (heel-raise) to 9.2 kcal (push-up)**; per minute presumably, unit wording as in the abstract. — [Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:32379233&resultType=core&format=json)
- **Vezina JW, Der Ananian CA, Campbell KD, Meckes N, Ainsworth BE (2014)** J Strength Cond Res 28(4):1026-1031. DOI 10.1519/jsc.0000000000000375, PMID 24402448. Twelve trained men did a bodyweight circuit (push-ups, curl-ups, pull-ups, lunges). A "nontraditional" calculation gave significantly higher EE for all activities (p < 0.001). **Three of four exercises reached vigorous intensity with the nontraditional method, none with the traditional one**. Method choice therefore flips the MET category. (Exact MET values not in the abstract.) Ainsworth, the Compendium lead, is a co-author. — [Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:24402448&resultType=core&format=json)

### Inferences
- For a traditional, self-paced gym session of 8-15 reps with 1-3 min rest, measured whole-session values of ~4-5 METs fall between Compendium 02054 (3.5) and 02052 (5.0). None of the non-circuit measured sessions found reached 6.0.
- Using 02050 = 6.0 for "heavy" sessions is defensible only as "the Compendium category label for powerlifting/bodybuilding vigorous effort". The directly measured studies found do not show heavier traditional sessions reaching 6 METs aerobically. Zanuso's 80% 3×8 condition gave 5.0 in young adults.
- Bodyweight 3.0 (02056) is consistent with measured values in older adults (2.1-3.8). Young adults doing push-ups at a cadence can exceed it (Nakagata 2022; Vezina 2014).

### Gaps
- The source studies behind Compendium 02050 (Taylor Code 210) and 02054 were not retrieved. The Compendium website page returned 404. It is unknown whether 6.0 comes from direct calorimetry of powerlifting/bodybuilding sessions or from older questionnaire-derived coding.
- No calorimetry study of a modern "heavy, low-rep, long-rest" powerlifting session reporting METs *including* rest was found. The HS arm of João 2021 (90% 6×5, 116 min, 5.27 kcal/min) is the closest.

## Q3. How do rest intervals and training to failure affect average session METs?

### Takeaway
Shorter rest raises average VO2 and kcal/min, via higher density, supersets and circuits. Longer rest lowers the time-averaged rate but lengthens the session, so total kcal can go up. Training to failure with light loads raises per-set EE and anaerobic contribution, but EPOC partly equalises totals. Muscle mass matters more than rest interval for total EE.

### Cited Findings
- **Ratamess NA et al. (2007)** Eur J Appl Physiol 100(1):1-17. DOI 10.1007/s00421-007-0394-y, PMID 17237951. Eight trained men, 5 sets bench press at 75% 1RM × 10 or 85% × 5, rest 30 s / 1 / 2 / 3 / 5 min. Mean VO2 and ventilation rose as rest shortened. For 5-rep sets, VO2 with 30-s rest was higher than with 2, 3 and 5 min, and 1 min was higher than 5 min. 30-s and 1-min rest caused **15-55% reductions in load/volume**. VO2, VE and RER stayed elevated through 30 min post-exercise. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00421-007-0394-y); [Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:17237951&resultType=core&format=json)
- **Farinatti PT, Castinheiras Neto AG (2011)** J Strength Cond Res 25(11):3181-3190. DOI 10.1519/jsc.0b013e318212e415, PMID 21993043. Ten men, leg press vs. chest fly, 1-min vs. 3-min rest. VO2 was higher for leg press than chest fly during exercise and EPOC regardless of rest. 1-min rest increased accumulated VO2 for leg press only. "Total EE was mainly influenced by muscle mass (p < 0.001)." — [Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:21993043&resultType=core&format=json)
- **Kelleher et al. (2010)**: supersets (reduced rest) gave **34.70 vs. 26.28 kJ/min**, ≈ +32% (derived), plus larger EPOC. — [Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:20300020&resultType=core&format=json)
- **Mitchell et al. (2024)**: EE during recovery periods between sets and exercises is substantial "despite no external work being performed". The highest measured METs (up to 8) occur in circuit training with minimal rest. — [PMC11393209](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11393209/)
- **Brunelli et al. (2019)**, to failure: light-load failure (30% 1RM, ~21-36 reps/set) gave higher exercise EE and lactic contribution than heavy-load failure (80%, ~7-9 reps). EPOC was similar and total EE did not differ. — [PLOS One](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0224801)
- **Haddock BL, Wilkin LD (2006)** Int J Sports Med 27(2):143-148. DOI 10.1055/s-2005-865601, PMID 16475061. Fifteen trained women, 9 exercises to volitional fatigue at 8RM, 1 set vs. 3 sets. Net exercise EE **661.9 ± 43.9 kJ (3 sets) vs. 234.7 ± 13.4 kJ (1 set)**. 120-min recovery EE was **not different (93.3 vs. 94.1 kJ)**. — [Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:16475061&resultType=core&format=json)
- **Scott et al. (2011, APNM)**: in a second set to fatigue, work fell at 70% and 80% 1RM but not at 90%, and anaerobic EE was lower in set 2 in all protocols. — [doi.org](https://doi.org/10.1139/h10-093)

### Inferences
- If the app wants a rule that tracks measured MET, "short rest / density" is the variable with the clearest direction: shorter rest gives higher mean VO2. The Compendium already encodes this as 02055 (5.8) for circuit/supersets.
- Because the app multiplies MET by the *whole-session* duration, long rests already add kcal through time. Measured data suggest long-rest heavy sessions have a *lower* mean MET. Assigning them 6.0 would therefore compound the overestimate: high MET × long duration.
- Volume (number of sets) drives total exercise EE almost linearly (Haddock 2006: 3 sets ≈ 2.8× the kJ of 1 set). This is handled automatically by session time in the app formula.

### Gaps
- Ratamess 2007 absolute VO2 values per rest condition were not extracted; only the abstract was accessible.
- No study reporting session METs vs. reps-in-reserve (e.g., RIR 0 vs. RIR 4) at a fixed load was found.

## Q4. Published regression/prediction equations for resistance-exercise EE

### Takeaway
Several equations exist. Each one predicts EE from **mechanical work variables** (load × displacement, total volume) or **body size/composition**, not from %1RM. All are exercise- or protocol-specific, and none gives a MET-level classification rule.

### Cited Findings
- **Robergs RA, Gordon T, Reynolds J, Walker TB (2007)** J Strength Cond Res 21(1):123-130. DOI 10.1519/00124278-200702000-00023, PMID 17313290. Twenty-three (bench press) and 20 (parallel squat) trained men, 5-min steady-state bouts. Bench press: **Y′ = −0.132 + 0.031(X1) + 0.01(X2), R² = 0.728, Sxy = 0.16**. Squat: **Y′ = −1.424 + 0.022(X1) + 0.035(X2), R² = 0.656, Sxy = 0.314**. Y′ = VO2 (units believed L/min, not verified), X1 = load (kg), X2 = displacement (cm). Caveat: the Europe PMC abstract prints the bench-press intercept as "+0.132" while other snippets print "−0.132"; verify against the PDF before citing. The authors report caloric costs higher than earlier methods. — [Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:17313290&resultType=core&format=json); [JSCR](https://journals.lww.com/nsca-jscr/Abstract/2007/02000/ENERGY_EXPENDITURE_DURING_BENCH_PRESS_AND_SQUAT.23.aspx)
- **Lytle JR, Kravits DM, Martin SE, Green JS, Crouse SF, Lambert BS (2019)** Med Sci Sports Exerc 51(7):1532-1537. DOI 10.1249/mss.0000000000001925, PMID 30768553. Fifty-two subjects (27 M, 25 F), 7 machine exercises at **60-70% 1RM**. Total net kcal = **0.874(height, cm) − 0.596(age, y) − 1.016(fat mass, kg) + 1.638(lean mass, kg) + 2.461(TV × 10⁻³) − 110.742**, R² = 0.773, SEE = 28.5 kcal. TV = total volume (kg), i.e. sets × reps × load. The TV term and constant come from the search-engine snippet of the abstract; Europe PMC's summary truncated them and gave "R = 0.773". Per-lift equations had R ≈ 0.62-0.83. — [MSSE/Ovid](https://www.ovid.com/jnls/acsm-msse/abstract/10.1249/mss.0000000000001925~predicting-energy-expenditure-of-an-acute-resistance?redirectionsource=fulltextview); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/331134846_Predicting_Energy_Expenditure_of_an_Acute_Resistance_Exercise_Bout_in_Men_and_Women)
- **Qiao Z et al. (2026)** J Strength Cond Res. DOI 10.1519/jsc.0000000000005368, PMID 42017977. Fifty-one participants (31 M), Smith-machine squats. Aerobic + glycolytic + EPOC measured. Best model: body weight + training load + HR (**R² = 0.885**). Simplified model: body weight + training weight (**R² = 0.847**). "Oxygen-uptake-based measurements underestimated squat intensity". Women expended less than men, with similar mechanical efficiency. — [Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:42017977&resultType=core&format=json)
- **Vianna JM et al. (2011)** J Hum Kinet 29A:75-82. DOI 10.2478/v10078-011-0062-5, PMID 23486188. At 12-24% 1RM, OMNI-RES RPE predicted energy cost with R > 0.97 and error < 0.2 kcal/min for triceps extension, lat pull-down and bench press. — [Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:23486188&resultType=core&format=json)
- **Wilmore et al. (1978)** gave no regression, but normalising circuit EE to lean body weight removed sex differences. — [Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:692305&resultType=core&format=json)

The full text was not accessible (403) and there are no MET values here. — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/11/18/8773); [TMU record](https://hub.tmu.edu.tw/en/publications/modeling-of-metabolic-equivalents-mets-during-moderate-resistance)

### Inferences
- Every validated predictor found uses absolute load × distance, total volume, body size and lean mass, or HR/RPE. None uses load *relative to the lifter's own best* (%1RM or e1RM/Best1RM). If the app wants a load-sensitive term with published support, an absolute-volume term (Lytle 2019) is closer to the evidence than an Effort Ratio threshold. However, Lytle predicts total kcal, not METs, and applies only to 60-70% 1RM machine work.

## Q5. Is whole-session averaging (including rest) appropriate for Compendium MET application? Limitations (anaerobic, EPOC, VO2 underestimation)

### Takeaway
Several calorimetry studies used to benchmark resistance-training METs compute METs over the training phase **including inter-set rest** (Zanuso 2016; Wilmore 1978 circuits with a 2:1 work:rest ratio; Phillips & Ziuraitis 2003, whole protocol). Whole-session averaging is therefore consistent with how measured MET values are typically reported. All VO2-based MET values share one limitation: they miss glycolytic energy during sets and ignore EPOC, so they tend to underestimate true cost. How much depends on the method, and the underestimate is larger for heavy/intense sets.

### Cited Findings
- **Zanuso et al. (2016)** method: oxygen consumption in the training phase "including rest between sets" was averaged to mL/kg/min and divided by 3.5 to obtain METs. — [PMC4763546](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4763546/)
- **Mitchell et al. (2024)**: indirect calorimetry "is unable to quantify the glycolytic contribution". Lactate conversion is commonly **3 mL O2/kg/mmol La⁻**. EPOC windows in the literature span **3-180 min**. Example: bench press to failure at 70% 1RM had **glycolytic 26.5 ± 4.4 kJ vs. aerobic 4.9 ± 2.0 kJ** during the set. Half-squat vs. biceps curl to failure at 80% 1RM: **150.9 ± 20.9 vs. 35.8 ± 9.5 kJ/min**. The review notes the "almost twofold difference" between the highest and lowest Compendium resistance METs and the need to define resistance exercise clearly. — [PMC11393209](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11393209/)
- **Vianna et al. (2011)**: anaerobic share of **64-87%** of set energy at 80% 1RM (AOD method). — [Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:23487002&resultType=core&format=json)
- **Scott et al. (2011, JSCR)**: anaerobic and recovery (EPOC) EE each significantly exceeded aerobic EE for single sets to fatigue. — [Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:20703175&resultType=core&format=json)
- **Vezina et al. (2014)**: the calculation method alone moved 3 of 4 bodyweight exercises from sub-vigorous to vigorous. — [Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:24402448&resultType=core&format=json)
- **Qiao et al. (2026)**: "oxygen-uptake-based measurements underestimated squat intensity". — [Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:42017977&resultType=core&format=json)
- EPOC magnitudes measured: Brunelli 2019 **~91-95 kcal** (vs. 75.8 control, in the post window used). Haddock 2006 **~93-94 kJ over 120 min**, independent of 1 vs. 3 sets. Kelleher 2010 **59.7-79.4 kJ**. Rustaden 2020: RMR elevated **15-22%** two hours post-exercise. — sources as cited above.

### Inferences
- Using (MET − 1) × 3.5 × kg / 200 × whole-session minutes, rest included, matches how Zanuso and Phillips derived their session METs. It can be defended as "consistent with measured-session methodology". Subtracting 1 MET (net) additionally removes resting cost, which the source MET values (gross) include.
- The glycolytic/EPOC underestimate is the one argument in favour of a higher MET for heavy or high-effort sessions. But the studies that did quantify anaerobic plus EPOC energy (Scott 2011, Brunelli 2019, João 2021) still did not find heavy loads more costly per minute or in total than lighter loads to failure. So "missing anaerobic energy" supports a higher MET for **effortful/to-failure** work generally, not specifically for heavy %1RM.

### Gaps
- No primary source was retrieved stating the Compendium's own averaging convention for resistance-training codes (e.g., whether the 02054/02050 source measurements included rest). Herrmann et al. 2024 methods text was not checked for this point.
